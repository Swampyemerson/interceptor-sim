Thrust and thrust-to-weight

Where the thrust number comes from

  • Planning value: 1.5 kg per motor, 6.0 kg static total. This is the repo's sizing number (hardware_order_list.md §3). I kept it for the ECO II because it is the low end of what the listings claim, so it is the conservative choice.
  • Retailer max for ECO II 2207 1900KV on 6S: 1850 g and 2020 g vendor. The listings do not say which prop or how fresh the pack was, so treat these as an upper bound.
  • Cross-check from a similar motor: FlashHobby K2207.5 1900KV on a Gemfan 5040 prop at about 23.6 V, full throttle: 1406 g, 28.4 A, 669 W, 2.10 g/W vendor. Same stator class and KV, so 1.4 to 2.0 kg per motor is the honest range.
  • How it gets measured: a $20 thrust rig, motor mounted upside down on a kitchen scale, reading thrust (g) and current (A) at throttle steps. That replaces all of the above.

The arithmetic

T/W = (4 x thrust per motor) / AUW

Planning, 800 g:   (4 x 1500 g) / 800 g  = 7.5 : 1
Retailer max:      (4 x 2020 g) / 800 g  = 10.1 : 1
Design ceiling:    (4 x 1500 g) / 950 g  = 6.3 : 1
Design rule:       T/W >= 5 : 1 at the ceiling weight

Why T/W matters for this vehicle

  • Thrust has to hold the aircraft up and push it sideways. Tilted by θ with full thrust T: vertical part T cos θ must equal W. Steepest tilt that still holds altitude: θ = arccos(1 / (T/W)), about 82° at 7.5:1.
  • Most sideways acceleration at constant altitude: a = g √((T/W)² − 1), about 7.4 g on paper. In practice the autopilot tilt limit sets it: PX4's default MPC_TILTMAX_AIR is 45°, which gives a = g tan 45° = 9.8 m/s². The flight plan uses 10 m/s² for the dash.
  • The extra thrust is control margin. A quad near full throttle cannot correct attitude because no motor can speed up further. High T/W means the dash and the terminal turn never saturate the motors.

Hover current and hover time

Momentum theory (actuator disk) gives the ideal power to hover. Divide by the prop's figure of merit and the motor and ESC efficiency to get electrical power, then by pack voltage to get current.

Worked at 800 g, sea level, 5.0 in props

W        = m g = 0.800 x 9.81             = 7.85 N
T/rotor  = W / 4                          = 1.96 N  (200 g)
A        = π (0.127/2)²                   = 0.01267 m²
v_i      = √( T / (2 ρ A) )               = 7.95 m/s   induced velocity
P_ideal  = T v_i per rotor = 15.6 W, x4   = 62.4 W
           (same thing: P = T^1.5 / √(2ρA))
P_elec   = P_ideal / (FM x η)
         = 62.4 / (0.50 x 0.75)           = 166 W
I_hover  = P_elec / V = 166 / 22.2        = 7.5 A
t_hover  = (0.80 x 1.5 Ah) / 7.5 A        = 0.16 h  = 9.6 min
Hover throttle ≈ √(W / T_max) = √(1/7.5)  = 37 %
  • Answer to give: about 7 to 8 A hover current and about 10 minutes of hover on a 1500 mAh 6S pack at 800 g, as an estimate derived. With FM 0.6 and 85% drive efficiency it is 5.5 A and 13 min, so the range is roughly 10 to 13 min.
  • The inputs I am least sure of: figure of merit (FM, how close the prop gets to ideal; small FPV props are around 0.4 to 0.6) and motor plus ESC efficiency at low throttle (about 0.75 to 0.85) estimate.
  • Why only 80% of the pack: landing at about 3.5 V per cell under load leaves roughly 20% in the pack, which protects LiPo life.
  • What scales it: hover power goes as weight to the 1.5 power. 10% more weight costs about 15% more power. That is why the target's placard (+32% weight) costs it 25 to 35% of its flight time (repo: placard_mount.md §4.6).
  • How it gets measured: hover for a minute, read current from the PM02 in the PX4 log, and read the learned hover throttle (PX4 MPC_THR_HOVER, ArduPilot MOT_THST_HOVER on the target).
  • Flying is not hovering. A dash at full throttle draws on the order of 100+ A (4 motors at 28 to 45 A each), which would empty the usable pack in under a minute. The plan budgets 30 to 60 s of standby hover before each dash.

Why 6S instead of 4S: for the same power, higher voltage means lower current. Resistive loss in the pack, wires and ESC goes as I²R, so half the current is a quarter of the heat, and voltage sag under a hard dash is smaller. It also lets both aircraft share one pack type and one charger.

Motor and prop constants

KV                 = 1900 RPM per volt (no load)
No-load RPM, full  = 1900 x 25.2 V           = 47,900 RPM
No-load RPM, nom.  = 1900 x 22.2 V           = 42,200 RPM
Loaded full RPM    ≈ 35,000 RPM   (estimate, ~80% of no-load at sagged voltage)

Torque constant    Kt = 60 / (2π KV)         = 0.00503 N·m/A
Shaft torque       τ ≈ Kt (I − I_noload)
Tip speed at 35k   = (35,000/60) x 2π x 0.0635 = 233 m/s  (Mach 0.68)
Pitch speed at 35k = 4.3 in x 35,000 RPM      = 64 m/s
Hover RPM          ≈ 35,000 x √(200 g / 1500 g) ≈ 12,800 RPM
                     = 213 Hz shaft, 640 Hz blade pass (3 blades)
  • Prop coefficients: thrust T = CT ρ n² D⁴ and power P = CP ρ n³ D⁵ (n in rev/s, D in m). Figure of merit FM = T1.5 / (P √(2ρA)). These come off the thrust rig, not a listing.
  • Pitch speed is how far the prop would screw forward per second with no slip. Thrust falls toward zero as airspeed approaches it, which is why static thrust overstates top speed.
  • Why the hover RPM matters: the motor rotation and blade-pass frequencies put vibration into the gyro. Flight controllers notch-filter those frequencies, ideally using RPM reported by the ESC (bidirectional DShot, which AM32 ESCs can send).
  • Repo note: if motors or ESC run hot on the bench, the fallback was 1755 KV and/or a lower-pitch prop. We already chose the 5x4.3x3 as the cooler-running prop.

Drag, trim and air density

Drag area (Cd x A) of a bare 5 in quad ≈ 0.014 m²   (assumption)
  back-solved from a typical ~30° nose-down trim at 20 m/s
Drag  D = ½ ρ V² (Cd A)
  at 9 m/s  (target speed)  = 0.69 N
  at 16 m/s (dash speed)    = 2.20 N
Steady trim pitch  tan θ = D / W   (800 g, W = 7.85 N)
  at 9 m/s  ≈ 5.1°
  at 16 m/s ≈ 15.6°   (more while accelerating)
  • Flat plate drag coefficient Cd ≈ 1.17 (square plate face-on). This is why the target's 450 mm AprilTag panel is mounted edge-on to the airflow: face-on it would make 11.8 N of drag at 9 m/s, 1.5 times the target's weight. Edge-on it adds 0.28 N, 42 times less (placard_mount.md §4.3) derived.
  • Air density by elevation (ISA standard day): sea level 1.225, 975 m about 1.11, Boulder at 1655 m about 1.04 kg/m³. Hot days are lower still.
  • What density does: max thrust scales with ρ, so about 15% less in Boulder. Ideal hover power scales with 1/√ρ, so about 8% more in Boulder. T/W measured at sea level is optimistic at altitude.
  • Disk loading at 800 g: W / (4A) = 155 N/m². Higher disk loading means higher induced velocity and more power to hover.

Carbon fiber

The interview answer to "what is the Young's modulus of carbon fiber" is "which carbon fiber?" There are three different numbers and they differ by a factor of four.

WhatYoung's modulusTier
The fiber alone (Toray T300 or T700S, standard modulus)230 GPavendor
Unidirectional laminate along the fibers (60% fiber, epoxy)134 to 140 GPavendor
Same laminate across the fibers (E2)≈ 8 to 10 GPaestimate
Quasi-isotropic plate, like a frame plate (in-plane)≈ 50 to 60 GPaderived
Aluminum 6061-T6, for comparison69 GPavendor
 
Quasi-isotropic rule of thumb (from lamination theory):
  E_QI ≈ 3/8 E1 + 5/8 E2 = 3/8 (135) + 5/8 (9) ≈ 56 GPa

Fiber strength: T300 3.53 GPa, T700S 4.90 GPa
Density:   carbon/epoxy ≈ 1.55 to 1.60 g/cm³,  aluminum 2.70 g/cm³
Specific stiffness E/ρ:  QI carbon ≈ 56/1.55 = 36,  aluminum 69/2.70 = 26  (MN·m/kg)
  • So what: a quasi-isotropic plate is a bit less stiff than aluminum but about 40% stiffer per kilogram, and much stronger per kilogram. That is why frames are carbon.
  • TBS does not publish the fiber grade or layup for the Source One plates, so 56 GPa is an estimate for the frame, not a spec.
  • It conducts electricity. Carbon fiber is a conductor (T300 resistivity about 1.7 x 10⁻⁵ Ω·m). A bare wire touching the frame can short to it. That is the likely cause of the I²C bus fault that killed the target's first flight controller. It is also why a nicked motor winding can short a phase to the whole frame.
  • Galvanic corrosion: carbon is noble relative to aluminum, so aluminum touching carbon in moisture corrodes. Relevant for aluminum standoffs and motor mounts.

Arm check: deflection, stress, vibration

Each arm treated as a cantilever beam with the motor at the tip. Arm thickness is the vendor's 6 mm. Width and free length are guesses until you measure the arm with calipers.

Formulas: I = b h³ / 12. Tip deflection δ = F L³ / (3 E I). Root bending stress σ = F L (h/2) / I. First bending mode f = (1/2π) √(3EI / (L³ m)), arm mass ignored, root assumed rigid. Compare f with the hover rotor frequency (about 213 Hz). If they are close, that resonance shows up as gyro noise, which is a reason to measure it.

Likely questions, short answers

What is your thrust-to-weight and how did you get it?

About 7.5 to 1 planned. Four motors at a conservative 1.5 kg each is 6 kg of static thrust, over an estimated 800 g all-up weight. The thrust is a listing-class number, not measured yet. The design rule was at least 5 to 1 at a 950 g ceiling, and the thrust rig plus a scale will replace both numbers.

How long can it hover, and how did you calculate it?

About 10 minutes. Momentum theory gives 62 W ideal hover power at 800 g. Dividing by a figure of merit of 0.5 and 75% motor and ESC efficiency gives about 166 W, which is 7.5 A at 22.2 V. Using 80% of 1.5 Ah gives 1.2 Ah over 7.5 A, about 9.6 minutes. The power module will log the real hover current.

Why 6S?

Same power at lower current, so less I²R loss and less voltage sag on a hard dash. It is the standard 5 inch power system, and both aircraft share one pack type and charger.

Why a 5 inch and not something smaller?

Payload. The onboard compute and camera stack is roughly 200 to 280 g, which is about the weight of an entire 2.5 inch quad. A small airframe carrying that loses its thrust margin. A 5 inch is the smallest that carries the full stack and keeps T/W above 5.

What sets hover current?

Weight to the 1.5 power, divided by the square root of air density times disk area, then the prop's figure of merit and the motor and ESC efficiency, then pack voltage. Bigger props, lighter aircraft and denser air all reduce it.

What is the Young's modulus of carbon fiber?

Depends which level. The fiber is about 230 GPa. A unidirectional laminate is about 135 GPa along the fibers and under 10 across. A quasi-isotropic plate like a frame is around 50 to 60 GPa, a bit below aluminum's 69 but stiffer per kilogram.

How much can it accelerate sideways?

On paper g √((T/W)² − 1), about 7 g. In practice the autopilot's tilt limit sets it. At PX4's default 45° max tilt that is 1 g, and the flight plan uses 10 m/s² for the dash.

What would you measure first when the build is done?

Weight on a scale, then thrust and current per motor on the rig, then a one-minute hover to log real hover current and the learned hover throttle. Those three numbers replace every estimate on this page.

Anything that surprised you on the hardware side?

Carbon fiber conducts. A crossed, over-stripped wire in a tightly packed build shorted the I²C bus on the target's flight controller. I traced it with a multimeter (1.4 V on SCL and SDA where 3.3 V belonged) and replaced the board.
