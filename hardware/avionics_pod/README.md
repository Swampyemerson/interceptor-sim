# Avionics pod — Pixhawk 6C Mini + Pi 5 + AI HAT+ (v1, NOT yet printed or fit-checked)

One removable module that carries the flight controller, the Pi and the accelerator, and
comes off the frame with one screw. Generated by `pod.py` (CadQuery); every dimension is a
named number at the top of that file. `preview.png` is a rough render.

**Status: revision 2 (2026-11-09). The Pixhawk is now sized and placed from Holybro's own
CAD; the frame numbers are still unmeasured. Nothing here has touched the real frame.**
Print `dock_coupon.stl` first (5 minutes, 0.8 mm thick) and check it against the top plate.

## The idea

| Part | Where it lives | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `dock` | bolted to the frame's top plate, stays there | wedge hook at the nose, two locating pins, one M3 threaded boss at the tail |
| `pod_base` | the removable module | flight controller on the floor, Pi 5 on four bosses above it, HAT on the Pi's own standoffs |
| `pod_lid` | on top of the HAT | vented roof and roll-over rails |

**To remove:** undo the one rear screw, lift the tail, slide back out of the hook.
**To refit:** toe in under the hook, hinge down onto the pins, one screw. The pins and the
wedge put it back in the same place each time, which matters because the flight controller's
orientation is calibrated to the frame.

## Fixing the dock to the frame: two ways

1. **No drilling, no hole pattern needed (default for now):** thin foam tape (VHB-type,
   about 1 mm) under the dock, plus two zip ties round the top plate. The ties lie in the two
   grooves across the dock's top face and drop through the notches at its edges, so the pod
   still sits flat. Tape on plastic and carbon is fine — the rule is no tape on a circuit board.
   Use THIN tape: thick soft foam lets the whole pod rock, and the flight controller inside it
   would rock with it. The Pixhawk already has its own vibration isolation inside the case.
2. **Bolted:** four M3 flat-head screws through the slotted holes, once the top plate's hole
   spacing is measured (`DOCK_HOLE_DX`, `DOCK_HOLE_DY`). The slots can simply be left unused.

Either way, the only number the dock really needs from the frame is the flat width of the top
plate (`DOCK_W`, assumed 38 mm).

## Why it is laid out this way

- **Flight controller at the bottom.** Its cables (power module, GPS, ESC signal, receiver,
  bench USB) leave through the two low side windows and the open front and back, right at
  top-plate level and below the Pi. It is also the part that most wants to be near the
  centre of mass.
- **Pi above, SD-card end pointing aft.** The SD card and power button are at the open tail;
  USB and Ethernet face the nose; USB-C power and HDMI are on the open side above the side
  panel. Nothing covers a Pi port.
- **The Pi-to-Pixhawk link (TELEM2) stays inside the pod**, so popping the pod off means
  unplugging only the cables that leave the pod.
- **Airflow by leaving walls out.** It is a rack, not a sealed box: the flight-controller
  bay is open on four sides, the gap between Pi and HAT is open all round, the roof is
  slotted. The price is less crash protection than a closed shell.
- **No adhesive touches a circuit board.** Pi and HAT are held by their four corner holes
  only. Foam tape goes under the Pixhawk's plastic case, nowhere else.

## Numbers from the model (`mass_report.json`)

- Printed mass (PETG, estimate): dock 13 g, pod base 20 g, lid 6 g.
- Removable module with boards: about 163 g, using estimated board weights.
- Its centre of mass: 6 mm aft of the Pi's centre, on the centreline, 34 mm above the top plate.
  **Place the dock so that point sits over the frame's centre** (x = 0 in the model is the
  Pi's centre; the coupon has a small hole there).
- Height: 61 mm above the top plate. Footprint 107 x 56 mm.

The height is the cost of stacking three boards. It raises the aircraft's centre of mass;
by how much depends on where the battery sits, so re-do the plumb-line test after fitting.

## Measure before printing the real parts

| # | Measure | Parameter in `pod.py` | Value assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flat width of the top plate where the dock sits | `DOCK_W` | 38 mm |
| 2 | Spacing of four M3 holes/slots in the top plate you can use | `DOCK_HOLE_DX`, `DOCK_HOLE_DY` | 30.5 x 30.5 mm (a guess) |
| 3 | Pixhawk 6C Mini case length, width, height | `FC_L`, `FC_W`, `FC_H` | 53.3 x 38.4 x 16.5 mm |
| 4 | Height of the tallest plug on top of the Pixhawk, cable bent over | `FC_PLUG_ROOM` | 4 mm (Model A has no top plugs; confirmed by the builder) |
| 5 | Height of the tallest part on top of the HAT | `LID_STANDOFF` | 8 mm |
| 6 | **Height of the prop tips above the top plate** | (check only) | must be well below 29 mm |
| 7 | Where the battery rides (on top or underneath) | (layout) | assumed underneath |

Item 6 matters: seen from above, the corners of the Pi sit at the edge of the prop discs
(computed for a 240 mm wheelbase and 5 inch props: -0.2 mm). That is true of any centred
Pi 5 on this frame. The Pi board is 29 mm above the top plate in this design; it is only
safe if the props spin clearly below that.

Item 7 matters too: if the battery straps on top of the frame, this pod is in its place and
the layout needs rethinking before anything is printed.

Which face of the Pixhawk carries the USB-C port, SD slot and servo pins was NOT verified;
the bay is open on all sides so any orientation is reachable. The flight controller must
still point the way `SENS_BOARD_ROT` says, or that parameter must be changed and re-verified.

## Hardware

- Dock to frame: 1 mm foam tape + 2 zip ties (up to 4.8 mm wide), OR 4 x M3 flat-head screws + nuts
- 1 x M3 heat-set insert + 1 x M3 x 10 screw or thumbscrew (rear release)
- 4 x M2.5 heat-set inserts (Pi bosses), 4 x M2.5 x 16 standoffs (come with the HAT),
  4 x M2.5 x 8 male-female standoffs + 4 x M2.5 screws (lid)
- 2 mm foam tape under the Pixhawk

## Printing

PETG (or ASA), 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters, 40 % infill. `dock` and `pod_base` print flat
side down with no supports (the hook and the window tops are 45 degree faces). `pod_lid`
prints upside-down relative to the model (flat face on the bed, rails up) — rotate as needed.

## Regenerate

```
python3 -m venv cadenv && cadenv/bin/pip install cadquery matplotlib numpy-stl
cadenv/bin/python hardware/avionics_pod/pod.py      # STL + STEP + mass_report.json
cadenv/bin/python hardware/avionics_pod/preview.py  # preview.png
```

`pod.py` refuses to export a part that is not a single valid solid. Checked for v1: zero
overlap between dock and pod, and between the Pixhawk/Pi volumes and the printed parts.
The STEP files open in SolidWorks, Fusion, Onshape or FreeCAD for hand edits.
For inspection open `avionics_pod_assembly.step`: the three printed parts in flight
position plus plain blocks named `REF_*` standing in for the Pixhawk, Pi 5 and HAT.
Those blocks are envelopes from the parameter block, not real board models. Units are
millimetres; x points to the nose, z is up from the top plate. A STEP import has no
feature tree, so change dimensions in `pod.py` and regenerate.

## Revision 2 (2026-11-09): what changed

Builder confirmed: Model A (nothing on the top face), and the Pixhawk flies with its
servo-pin / MAIN / AUX end pointing FORWARD (arrow aft, `SENS_BOARD_ROT=4`). Rev 2 keeps
that, so no flight setting changes.

- Pixhawk sized from Holybro's STEP: 54.3 x 39.0 x 17.45 mm.
- Gap above the Pixhawk cut from 12 mm to 4 mm. The Pi sits 7 mm lower (29 mm, was 36),
  the whole pod is 7 mm shorter (61 mm) and its centre of mass 5 mm lower (29 mm).
- Rear release screw moved 14 mm aft: about 18 mm of plug-and-bend room behind the
  Pixhawk for POWER, TELEM, GPS and CAN. The dock grew from 93 to 107 mm long.
- Pixhawk moved 4 mm aft: 11 mm of floor then open air ahead of the servo pins. The
  plugs pass over the dock hook (hook top 8.5 mm, Pixhawk underside 7.4 mm).
- Corner fences lowered so they cannot block the bottom row of end connectors.
- Checked in CAD: a 15 mm keep-clear block at each end of the Pixhawk overlaps no
  printed part. The blocks are in the assembly as `REF_plug_room_*`.
- `pod.py --vendor-fc <6C-MINI-A_rev.stp>` puts Holybro's real case in the assembly.

Still unverified: top-plate flat width (38 mm assumed), the frame hole pattern, the
standoff above the HAT, real plug lengths, and the camera ribbon run (the builder
reports the ribbon leaves the camera about 3 inches above the frame, which would put
it near Pi height and make the reach easy; not measured).

## Vendor CAD and sourced dimensions (found 2026-11-09; this is what rev 1 got wrong)

Rev 1 was drawn from remembered datasheet numbers. Official models exist and
change the design. They are not committed here (vendor files); fetch them from:

- Pixhawk 6C Mini STEP, three variants (Model A legacy, Model A current, Model B):
  <https://docs.holybro.com/autopilot/pixhawk-6c-mini/download>
- Pixhawk 6C Mini port layout drawings:
  <https://docs.holybro.com/autopilot/pixhawk-6c-mini/pixhawk-6c-mini-ports>
- Raspberry Pi 5 STEP: <https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpi5/RaspberryPi5-step.zip>
- AI HAT+ mechanical drawing:
  <https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/ai-hat-plus.html>

Measured from those files (bounding boxes, CadQuery import, WSL dev machine):

| Item | Size (mm) | Rev 1 assumed |
|---|---|---|
| 6C Mini Model A, legacy | 53.3 x 39.0 x 16.2 | 53.3 x 38.4 x 16.5 |
| 6C Mini Model A, current | 54.3 x 39.0 x 17.45 | same |
| 6C Mini Model B | 58.3 x 39.0 x 18.15 | same |
| Pi 5 with connectors | 88.5 x 57.6 x 19.0 (board 85 x 56) | board 85 x 56 |
| AI HAT+ board | 65 x 56.5, holes 58 x 49 | 65 x 56 |

What the port drawings show, and what it breaks in rev 1:

- **No connector leaves the top of a Model A.** POWER, TELEM1, TELEM2, GPS1, CAN1
  and CAN2 leave the FRONT end (the end the arrow points to) in two rows; USB-C,
  FMU debug, I2C and GPS2 leave the left side; DSM, RSSI and the microSD the right
  side; the servo pin header, AUX5, AUX6 and RC IN leave the REAR end. On a Model B
  the servo pin header is on top instead. There is no TELEM3 port on this board.
- So `FC_PLUG_ROOM = 12` is wasted height on a Model A: the Pi could sit roughly
  9 mm lower. A Model B still needs it.
- The room rev 1 lacks is at the ENDS. The front floor stops about 11 mm past the
  FC's front face (the dock hook is there), and the rear release-screw boss sits
  about 8 mm behind its rear face, in line with the servo header. Rev 2 must give
  both ends plug-and-bend room, or turn the FC sideways.
- The FC currently flies nose-backwards (`SENS_BOARD_ROT=4`). Rev 2 must pick an
  orientation on purpose; changing it means changing that setting and re-checking.
- Camera ribbon: the Pi 5's camera sockets are on its top face near the middle of
  the board. Estimated run to a nose camera is 170-200 mm with the Pi at 36 mm
  (an estimate, not measured). The AR0234 ships a 150 mm cable. Lowering the Pi
  helps by under 20 mm; a 300 mm 22-pin-to-15-pin cable is the sure fix.
