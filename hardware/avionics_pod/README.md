# Avionics pod — Pixhawk 6C Mini + Pi 5 + AI HAT+ (v1, NOT yet printed or fit-checked)

One removable module that carries the flight controller, the Pi and the accelerator, and
comes off the frame with one screw. Generated by `pod.py` (CadQuery); every dimension is a
named number at the top of that file. `preview_nose_camera.png` is a rough render.

## Files in this folder: current design only

Every 3D file here belongs to the **current** revision. When a part is superseded, its
files are deleted in the same commit (git history keeps them), and `pod.py` no longer
writes them, so an old part cannot be printed by mistake. A test fails if anything else
appears.

## The idea

| Part | Where it lives | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `dock` | bolted to the frame's top plate, stays there | wedge hook at the nose, two locating pins, one M3 threaded boss at the tail |
| `pod_base` | the removable module | flight controller on the floor, Pi 5 on four bosses above it, HAT on the Pi's own standoffs |
| `pod_lid` | on top of the HAT | vented roof and roll-over rails |

**To remove:** undo the one rear screw, lift the tail, slide back out of the hook.
**To refit:** toe in under the hook, hinge down onto the pins, one screw. The pins and the
wedge put it back in the same place each time, which matters because the flight controller's
orientation is calibrated to the frame.

## Fixing the dock to the frame

**Bolted is now the plan** (changed 2026-11-09 after the vibration analysis and the gap
review; see `ANALYSIS.md` and `GAP_REVIEW.md`).

1. **Bolted (for flight):** TBS's published drawing of the top plate shows no 30.5 mm
   pattern. Its only holes are the standoff screws, 29.2 mm apart side to side. The dock's
   four holes now match four of those screws (55.77 mm apart fore-aft), so the dock goes on
   with four longer M3 flat-head screws into the frame's own standoffs. This is from the
   drawing, not from the real plate: print `dock_cam_coupon` and lay it on the plate first.
2. **Tape and zip ties (for a fit check only):** thin tape under the dock and two ties
   through the plate's strap slots. The analysis puts the whole pod's rocking on tape
   inside the motor vibration range for any tape, and that rocking carries the Pixhawk.
   Do not fly with the Pixhawk aboard on tape alone.

The top plate is 42.5 mm wide for only about 40 mm of its length and 29 mm wide elsewhere,
so the 38 mm dock overhangs the narrow parts. Its tail is narrowed to keep the corners away
from the rear prop tips.

## Why it is laid out this way

- **Flight controller at the bottom.** Its cables (power module, GPS, ESC signal, receiver,
  bench USB) leave through the two low side windows and the open front and back, right at
  top-plate level and below the Pi. It is also the part that most wants to be near the
  centre of mass.
- **Pi above, SD-card end pointing aft.** The SD card and power button are at the open tail;
  USB and Ethernet face the nose; USB-C power and HDMI are on the open side above the side
  panel. Nothing covers a Pi port.
- **The Pi-to-Pixhawk link (TELEM2) stays inside the pod**, so popping the pod off means
  unplugging only the cables that leave the pod.
- **Airflow by leaving walls out.** It is a rack, not a sealed box: the flight-controller
  bay is open on four sides, the gap between Pi and HAT is open all round, the roof is
  slotted. The price is less crash protection than a closed shell.
- **No adhesive touches a circuit board.** Pi and HAT are held by their four corner holes
  only. Foam tape goes under the Pixhawk's plastic case, nowhere else.

## Numbers from the model (`mass_report.json`)

- Printed mass (PETG, estimate): dock 13 g, pod base 20 g, lid 6 g.
- Removable module with boards: about 163 g, using estimated board weights.
- Its centre of mass: 6 mm aft of the Pi's centre, on the centreline, 34 mm above the top plate.
  **Place the dock so that point sits over the frame's centre** (x = 0 in the model is the
  Pi's centre; the coupon has a small hole there).
- Height: 61 mm above the top plate. Footprint 107 x 56 mm.

The height is the cost of stacking three boards. It raises the aircraft's centre of mass;
by how much depends on where the battery sits, so re-do the plumb-line test after fitting.

## Revision 5 (2026-11-11): camera post redrawn for the real top plate

**What was wrong.** Revision 4 took the end of the top plate with the narrow pair of
screws (22 mm apart) to be the nose, and ran the camera post's tongue out to that pair.
The builder's photo and caliper readings on the target drone (same frame) show the
narrow pair is at the rear, by the GPS. At the nose there is a full-width row of screws
25 mm ahead of the dock's front row (measured 25.05), and the plate ends 22 mm past it.

| Measured 2026-11-11 (centre to centre) | Calipers | Drawing |
|---|---|---|
| Dock's four screws, left to right | 29.3 mm | 29.2 |
| Dock's four screws, fore and aft | 56.0 mm | 55.77 |
| Dock's front row to the nose row | 25.05 mm | 25.0 |

**What changed in the CAD**

- **Nose screws:** now the frame's front row, 29.2 mm apart, 25 mm ahead of the dock's
  front row. They sit just behind the post, not in front of it.
- **Post moved 5 mm forward** (tilt axis at 65 mm) so its rear wall clears those screw
  heads. The tongue stops at 66.9 mm, just short of the plate's nose edge at 67.4.
- **Tongue 6 mm thick ahead of the pod** (was 3). With the screws behind the post, the
  tongue is what the post nods on; at 3 mm the nodding mode fell to 92 Hz with the dock
  held only at its screws, at 6 mm it is 187 Hz. The two nose screws need to be 14 mm
  long there, not 10.
- **Cable slot removed** from the dock and the pod floor. The plate's own cut-out turns
  out to be near the rear, under the Pixhawk's tail, so wires from below come up beside
  the 29 mm wide plate and in through the pod's side windows. Look at the real wires
  before printing; this is the least-checked change.
- **Motor positions corrected** in the clearance and balance checks. TBS's drawing puts
  the motors 77 mm fore and aft and 92 mm left and right of the frame centre, not 85 and
  85, and the frame centre midway between the dock's four screws.

## Revision 4 (2026-11-10): camera on a short post off the dock's nose

The builder picked this over the lid cradle and the nose boom. Two new printed parts and
one new template; everything else is unchanged. (The plain `dock` files were removed on
2026-11-11.)

| Part | What it is | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| `dock_cam` | the dock, plus a tongue along the top plate to the frame's front row of screws, plus two cheeks and two cross walls | 26.4 g (plain dock 12.8 g) |
| `cam_plate_nose` | the tilting plate the camera board screws to | 4.9 g |
| `dock_cam_coupon` | 0.8 mm template of `dock_cam`'s outline and all six holes: **print this first** | |

Open `avionics_pod_assembly_nose_camera.step` to inspect it; `preview_nose_camera.png` is
a rough picture.

**How it goes together**

1. Bolt `dock_cam` to the top plate with six longer M3 flat-head screws through the
   frame's own standoff holes: the four the plain dock uses, plus the front row (those
   two 14 mm long). Do this before the pod goes on: the front-row screws end up under
   the Pi's overhanging nose.
2. Press four M3 heat-set inserts into the plate's two ears (two each side).
3. Screw the camera board to the plate's four posts (M2). Mount it **ribbon connector
   up**: the plate's window is open at the top, and that is the short way to the Pi
   (about 100 mm; connector down is about 150 mm, the full length of the stock ribbon).
   The picture is then upside down and needs flipping in software. That flip is not
   written yet.
4. Fit the plate between the cheeks with two M3 pivot screws and two M3 lock screws.
   Set the tilt (10 to 45 degrees up), tighten all four.
5. Hinge the pod in as before. The post is clear of the Pi's USB block to at least
   12 degrees of tip.

**Numbers** (from `nose_post_check.py`, `fea_modal.py --nose`, `analysis.py`)

- Tilt axis 65 mm ahead of the pod datum, 36 mm above the top plate.
- First shake mode 258 Hz in the model with the dock held flat (218 Hz with softer
  plastic), against 136 Hz for the lid cradle. Held only at its six screws it is 187 Hz.
  See `ANALYSIS.md` section 2. The SOLIDWORKS check was on revision 4.
- Props stay out of the picture with the delivered 70 degree lens and with the AR0234 at
  every tilt in the range (+28 degrees or better at the shallowest). The published
  118 degree lens is marginal at 10 degrees of tilt (+12 / +9.5) and clear from 25.
- Whole aircraft 853 g, roll inertia +12 % over the old plan.

**What it costs**

- The camera stands in front of the Pi's USB and Ethernet ports. Nothing plugs in there
  with the camera plate fitted (two pivot and two lock screws to remove it).
- The camera plate stays 8.7 to 10 mm outside the front prop circles in top view, and 16
  to 21 mm above the blades. That rests on TBS's drawing for where the motors are and on
  the props being level with the top plate; neither is measured on the aircraft.
- The camera is again the first thing to touch the target, as on the nose boom.
- The cheeks print upright from the tongue with no supports; the rear wall leans 10
  degrees and the front edges 31 degrees, both inside what prints clean.

**Check on the real aircraft before flying it**

- Lay `dock_cam_coupon` on the plate: do all six holes line up, and does the tongue stop
  at the plate's nose edge?
- With the camera fitted, turn each front prop by hand through a full circle at the
  tilt you will fly. Then the corner check from the contract: props spinning, camera at
  a plain wall, look at the picture's corners.
- The camera plate is fitted to Arducam's own model of the B0353
  (`uctronics.com/download/Mechanical_Drawing/B0353.STEP`, not committed): 38 x 38 board,
  2.5 mm holes on a 34.04 mm square, ribbon connector on the back along one edge, and the
  lens holder's two screw heads on the back 10 mm either side of centre. Compare the
  delivered board against the printed plate before screwing it down. The board also has a
  row of seven unpopulated pin holes at the edge opposite the connector; if yours has pins
  soldered there, they will foul the plate's bottom bar.

## Revision 3b (2026-11-09 late): analysis and gap-review fixes

Read `ANALYSIS.md` (mass, vibration, heat) and `GAP_REVIEW.md` (14 ranked findings from a
second look at the CAD against the vendors' own drawings). Applied in `pod.py`:

- Dock holes moved to the frame's standoff screws; tie stations moved to the plate's
  strap slots; dock tail narrowed (it was 3.8 mm from the rear prop tips in plan).
- A cable slot through the dock and pod floor ahead of the Pixhawk, for wires coming up
  from the stack below.
- A taller notch in the right side panel for the Pixhawk's USB-C plug (it clashed by
  1.5 mm).
- The camera plate is open at its bottom edge for the camera's ribbon connector.
- The camera cradle's arms now run the lid's full length outside the lid screws, with a
  bridge bar: first shake mode 110 Hz to 138 Hz in the model. Still the softest place on
  the aircraft for a camera; `ANALYSIS.md` section 2 gives the alternatives.
- The vendor Pixhawk was drawn backwards in the assembly file (printed parts were not
  affected). Fixed; checked by slicing both end walls of Holybro's model.

Not CAD, but needed (from the gap review):

- **Pi-to-Pixhawk serial cable:** the AI HAT+ covers the GPIO pins and they do not poke
  through (builder, 2026-11-09), so the DuPont plugs cannot be used. Plan: the Pi 5's own
  3-pin serial socket between the two HDMI ports. It is top-entry, so it must be plugged
  in before the HAT goes on. It is a different port (`/dev/ttyAMA10`, not `/dev/ttyAMA0`)
  and is the boot console by default, so the flight code and Pi setup need a change, and
  it is untested here. It supports 921600 baud, the rate TELEM2 uses.
- **Pi power plug:** a straight USB-C plug sticks out over the rear-right prop. Use a
  right-angle plug pointing aft.
- **Standoffs:** the HAT kit's are reported to be nylon. Use brass M2.5 male-female,
  four 16 mm and four 8 mm, especially if a camera rides on the lid.
- **Camera ribbon:** needs one diagonal fold to reach the Pi's sockets; about 100 to
  110 mm of path, so a 150 mm cable reaches.
- **GPS and compass position** is not designed.

## Revision 3 draft (2026-11-09 overnight): camera on the pod, and tidier parts

**Is a camera on this pod possible? Yes, by geometry.** `camera_on_pod_fov.py` works out
how far outside the picture the front props sit. The props are fixed to the same body
as the camera, so the answer depends only on where the camera is and how far it is
tilted up, not on how the aircraft is flying.

| Camera | Margin at 10 deg tilt | at 25 deg | at 45 deg |
|---|---|---|---|
| OV9281 as delivered (70 deg lens, measured) | +29.5 deg | +44.5 | +64.5 |
| AR0234 stock lens (80 deg, vendor) | +25.5 | +40.5 | +60.5 |
| OV9281 published lens (118 deg) | +7 (marginal) | +22 | +42 |

Lens at 60 mm forward, 68 mm up from the top plate (the cradle as drawn). If the props
sit 10 mm higher than the builder's "level with the top plate", subtract about 4 deg.
Rule used: 10 deg or more is clear. Limits of this derivation: pinhole lens (no
distortion, which understates what a wide lens sees in the corners), pod assumed centred
on the frame, props treated as flat discs. It is a reason to print and test, not a pass:
the gate is still props spinning, camera at a plain wall, check the corners.

Why the pod may be the better place than a nose boom (each maps to the contract's
mount-risk checklist):

- Camera, Pi and Pixhawk are one rigid unit, so the camera-to-IMU angle survives taking
  the pod off and on.
- The ribbon is under 100 mm and never crosses a flexing boom.
- The camera is no longer the first thing to hit the target.
- The lever arm from the centre of mass is shorter, so less parallax to correct.

What could make it worse, and is NOT yet checked:

- **Flex.** The camera now sits on top of a 61 mm tower. A rough beam estimate puts the
  tower's sideways bending in the low hundreds of Hz, the same range as the motor shaft
  frequency in hover (about 213 Hz). That estimate is crude; it needs a tap test or a
  blur check on the printed part. Soft foam under the dock would make it far worse: with
  a camera on board the dock must be bolted, or tied down on thin tape.
- The lid rides on the Pi's standoff stack. Brass standoffs through-bolted are stiff in
  theory; the joints through two circuit boards are the unknown.
- Camera board hole spacing (34 mm) is a common Arducam value, not a published one.

**Not chosen; its files were removed on 2026-11-11.** `pod.py` still holds the geometry
for the vibration comparison. The parts were `pod_lid_cam` (the lid with two cheeks reaching forward over the USB ports)
and `cam_plate` (the tilting plate). One M3 screw each side is the pivot, a second each
side locks the tilt anywhere from 10 to 45 degrees. The cheeks overhang
the lid's front edge, so that part prints with supports. Checked in CAD at 10, 25 and
45 degrees: plate and camera touch neither the lid nor the USB ports, and the top USB
ports stay reachable. The plain `pod_lid` is unchanged if the camera stays on the nose.

Tidying in the same pass: rounded corners on the dock, pod floor and lid; a sunk arrow
on the dock and on the lid pointing to the nose; two zip-tie slots on the rear tab to
anchor the cables leaving aft. Suggested hardware: an M3 thumb screw for the rear
release, so the pod comes off without a tool.

What the port drawings show, and what it breaks in rev 1:

- **No connector leaves the top of a Model A.** POWER, TELEM1, TELEM2, GPS1, CAN1
  and CAN2 leave the FRONT end (the end the arrow points to) in two rows; USB-C,
  FMU debug, I2C and GPS2 leave the left side; DSM, RSSI and the microSD the right
  side; the servo pin header, AUX5, AUX6 and RC IN leave the REAR end. On a Model B
  the servo pin header is on top instead. There is no TELEM3 port on this board.
