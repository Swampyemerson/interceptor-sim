Three wires to the Pi, and one GPS that lives on the wrong port

Everything below is the Pixhawk 6C Mini talking to the Raspberry Pi 5 over one serial line. Nothing here can spin a motor — there is no airframe and no ESC.

First The cable, because DuPont does not fit

You were right to stop. TELEM2 is a JST-GH 1.25 mm 6-pin socket — the same tiny latching type as your GPS plug. A DuPont jumper cannot go into it; the pitch is less than half as wide. The repo said "three DuPont jumpers," which described the Pi end only. That wording has been corrected.

What you need at the flight-controller end is one of:

A JST-GH 6-pin to DuPont-female cable — sold as a Pixhawk telemetry-to-DuPont lead. Plugs into TELEM2, the loose ends push onto the Pi header.

MAIN and AUX are not serial ports

Those 3-pin S / + / − headers are the PWM motor outputs. They carry a servo signal and a 5 V rail. Wiring the Pi's GPIO to them puts 5 V on a 3.3 V pin and connects nothing useful.

Four ports look identical

POWER1, TELEM1, TELEM2 and GPS2 are all 6-pin JST-GH. They are distinguished only by the silkscreen label next to the socket. Read it before it clicks in — POWER1 carries the battery sense rail.

Confirm the orientation on your own board

Don't trust a drawing for which end is pin 1. Run pinout on the Pi — it ships with Pi OS and prints your actual board with the header mapped, so you can match it to what's in front of you before anything is pushed on.

5a Splicing a TELEM↔TELEM cable

Cutting a spare 6-pin TELEM cable and soldering Dupont pigtails onto three of its wires is a normal move. Two things make it worth doing carefully: five of the six wires are usually the same colour, and the colour convention is vendor discretion, not standard — one distributor warns in writing that the red wire may not be pin 1. So every identification here is measured, not counted, and the order below matters.

Check the mail before the scissors

A ready-made JST-GH → Dupont pigtail is already on order. Cutting pins 4 and 5 off a TELEM cable is permanent, so this donor can never carry a SiK radio afterwards. Hand-make one only to close the link tonight.

  1. Find GND with the meter, not the colours. FC unpowered, USB out, cable plugged into TELEM2. Continuity mode: one probe on the FC's USB shell, touch each wire at the far plug. The one that beeps is pin 6. Flag it within 10 mm of the plug boot.
    Pin order is only guaranteed at the boot. Probe the wires, never inside the 1.25 mm socket — a probe tip in there bridges pins.
  2. Find +5 V. Power the FC on USB only. DC volts, black probe on your flagged GND. The wire reading ≈5 V is pin 1. Flag it.
    If NOTHING reads 5 V, that is a legitimate result, not a mistake — the peripheral 5 V is a switched, current-limited rail and USB-only behaviour is undocumented for this board. Fall back to: pin 1 is the wire at the opposite ribbon edge from measured GND. Do not re-cut.
  3. Count inward from both measured ends. From pin 1: next is 2 = TX, then 3 = RX. From pin 6: next in is 5 = RTS, then 4 = CTS.
    Counting from an edge is flip-invariant — turning the cable over never reverses the sequence. Anchoring from opposite ends is what separates TX (pin 2) from RTS (pin 5): both are FC outputs and both read a clean logic level, so voltage alone cannot tell them apart.
  4. Cut. Then solder — heatshrink threaded on FIRST. It will not go on afterwards; the Dupont housing is too fat. Lap the strands side by side, one joint at a time, three different jumper colours.
    Only now is it safe to cut, because both anchors are already measured and flagged.
  5. Cap the three you don't use — +5 V, CTS, RTS — back to ~10 mm, each in its own heatshrink.
    Short leaves nothing to flop onto the Pi header. Pin 4 is an FC input, and TELEM pins are not documented as 5 V tolerant.
  6. Continuity sweep, unplugged and unpowered — all 15 pairs, capped stubs included.
    +5 V against TX, RX or GND is the pair that kills a GPIO or crowbars the FC rail. Continuity means nothing while the plug sits in a powered board.
  7. Wire to the Pi with everything dead. GND first, then RX, then TX. Remove GND last when dismantling.
    Pi header pin 6 (GND) is one pin from pin 4 (+5 V) — a one-pin slip dumps the Pi's 5 V into the FC's ground. And until GND is joined, two independently powered grounds float relative to each other.
  8. Prove it in two stages. GND + FC TX only → byte check. Then power down, add FC RX → MAVSDK check.
    Stage one involves no Pi output, so a wrong guess costs nothing. Bytes arriving is the only thing that actually proves a serial lead.

brn-02 The M10 on GPS2

No wiring for this one — the plug goes straight in. GPS2 is 6-pin JST-GH carrying a UART and an I²C bus, which is why a GPS-plus-compass module fits a single socket:

1VCC +5Vpower
2TX (out)gps
3RX (in)gps
4SCLi²c bus 2
5SDAi²c bus 2
6GNDground

Set GPS_1_CONFIG to "GPS 2" and SER_GPS2_BAUD to Auto, then reboot. Use GPS_1_CONFIG, not GPS_2_CONFIG — the latter leaves the primary GPS pointed at an empty port, failing forever.

The compass will not appear, and that is not a fault

PX4 v1.16 on this board starts the external magnetometer on I²C bus 1 (GPS1's) and bus 4 (internal) only. GPS2's I²C is bus 2, which is never scanned — so the M10's compass is invisible, looking exactly like a dead module. The bench uses the board's internal compass instead; a 6→10-pin cable moves the module to the scanned bus for flight. Full reasoning in ADR-0091.

Checks, in order — stop at the first failure

  1. In QGC's MAVLink Console (Analyze Tools), run gps status.
    # also useful, prints the live values:
listener sensor_gps
    Proves the driver found the receiver and identified its protocol. Indoors you will see few or zero satellites — that is the roof, not a wiring fault. A named device and a u-blox protocol line is the pass here; the satellite count is what you check outdoors.
  2. Free the Pi's serial port: sudo raspi-config → Interface Options → Serial Port → login shell No, serial hardware Yes. Reboot.
    ls -l /dev/serial0        # should point at ttyAMA0
grep -o 'console=serial0' /boot/firmware/cmdline.txt   # must print NOTHING
    By default Linux runs a login prompt on those pins and eats every MAVLink byte before your code sees it.
  3. Raw bytes on the wire.
    stty -F /dev/ttyAMA0 921600 && timeout 3 cat /dev/ttyAMA0 | xxd | head
    Hex scrolling proves the FC → Pi direction only. Silence means: wrong device, TX/RX not crossed, console still holding the port, or a baud mismatch.
  4. MAVSDK parses a heartbeat — this closes brn-04.
    .venv-seeker/bin/python -c "import asyncio; from mavsdk import System
async def m():
    d=System(); await d.connect(system_address='serial:///dev/ttyAMA0:921600')
    async for s in d.core.connection_state():
        print('connected', s.is_connected); break
asyncio.run(m())"
    Bytes are not just present but well-formed MAVLink. Still only one direction.
  5. The gate — this closes brn-05, the one link the simulation never exercised.
    scripts/check_deploy_bench.sh
    Connect → own-state → OFFBOARD accepted → a measured setpoint stream → clean stop, all disarmed. The advancing own-state stamp is the only evidence bytes went back up the wire, so this is where your Pi → FC lead is proven. It refuses to pass over USB and exits 3 instead.
If it connects but stalls in half-second chunks

That is hardware flow control on a three-wire link. Confirm MAV_1_FLOW_CTRL = 0 actually took, in QGC's Parameters page. The rest of the failure ladder, in likelihood order, is in configs/px4_6cmini/README.md.
